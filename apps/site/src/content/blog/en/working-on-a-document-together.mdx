Take an office with a housing scheme in the permit phase. Thursday is the site meeting with the building services consultants, and by then revision 4 of the ground floor layout has to be reviewed. Anna leads the project. On Tuesday morning she opens the document in the review, full screen, and sees that Miriam is already in it. Her pointer, with her name on it, sits at the stair core.

Until now a round like this usually ran on comments in a PDF, an email to the colleague, a second file with her comments coming back, and at the end a list for the minutes, typed up from both. In Piloti it happens in one document that everyone works in at the same time. Piloti works there too, as a colleague.

## Annotations where the question is

An annotation sits on one page of one revision. It is a point, or a region you draw. It has a kind (defect, question or note), a running number, optionally a person responsible and a due date, and a thread to answer in. It is open, resolved or discarded.

Anna draws a region around the shaft next to the stair core. The opening does not match what the building services consultants sent last. Question, Miriam responsible, due Wednesday. While Anna is still drawing, Miriam sees that she is marking a region, and the annotation appears on her screen as soon as it is set. Following a colleague means seeing what she sees, because your view moves with hers. If two people change the same annotation at the same moment, the review says so and shows where it stands now.

An annotation from the round on revision 3 stays in the list and says it was set on revision 3. It is not drawn on revision 4, because the place may have moved. Whoever reviews a lot stays on the keyboard. C picks the point, R the region, J and K step from annotation to annotation, E takes a proposal over.

## Asking Piloti about exactly this detail

Anna marks a region and asks Piloti about it. There are quick questions: “What is this?”, “Check dimensions”, “Check against OIB”, “Draft a query”. She takes the last one. Piloti looks at exactly that crop, and the answer appears beside the region, phrased as a query to the consultants. Anna keeps it as an annotation.

Piloti has its own pointer in the document. It points at the place it is writing about and highlights regions. When its answer is done, the pointer leaves. If Anna notices Piloti looking at the wrong place, she adds a note while it works: “not the door, the stair”. Piloti takes that into its next step instead of starting over. An annotation or reply it had already chosen waits for the note.

## What Piloti finds is a proposal

With “Have Piloti review”, Piloti goes through the whole document and sets what it finds at the places it concerns. These findings are proposals. They lead the list as a group you walk through one by one, taking each over or discarding it. Only an annotation someone took over belongs to the office.

Nobody has to remember that. The database will not store an open annotation by Piloti without a person behind it.

## What Piloti does when you ask

When Anna asks for it explicitly (“set an annotation there”, “resolve number 7”, “assign that to Miriam, due Wednesday”), Piloti does exactly that. It does it the way Anna could herself: with her rights, through the same paths her own clicks take. What Anna may not do, Piloti may not do for her. It can delete only annotations Anna set herself. You name the person who should be responsible. If nobody matches, or several people do, Piloti asks.

Every change to an existing annotation leaves a short note in its thread, for example “Resolved: the clear width is dimensioned in section B-B as 1.25 m.” Anyone who opens the annotation sees what happened and why.

Whether Piloti may do this is the office’s decision. Until it is switched on, Piloti only proposes: an annotation you ask for arrives as a proposal, and a change or a deletion is left to the person, in the document. That is how an office starts, and it lets Piloti do more once it trusts it.

## For whom Piloti writes

Everything Piloti writes in the document carries its name and the name of the person it works for: “Piloti · for Anna”, “Proposal by Piloti · for Anna”. When Piloti sets or resolves an annotation at Anna’s request, Anna is the one shown as responsible. She asked, so she stands behind it.

There are two reasons. Article 50 of the European AI Act, applicable since August 2026, requires transparency about where AI is involved. The second reason is everyday work. Whoever reads a reply in the thread on Thursday has to know whether Anna wrote it or Piloti on her behalf, and whom to ask if it is wrong.

What is in a thread, everyone who may open the document reads. If a conversation drew on a restricted folder or another project, Piloti writes nothing from it into the document.

## @Piloti in a thread, and delegating to Piloti

On Wednesday Anna is on site. Miriam writes in the thread of annotation 7: “@Piloti, is the clear width of the door dimensioned anywhere?” That wakes Piloti. It works for Miriam, looks at the document and answers in the thread, even when nobody has the chat open. Below the reply it says what woke it. Answering a question Piloti asked itself wakes it the same way.

You can also hand an open annotation to Piloti entirely, with “Delegate to Piloti” in the annotation’s menu. It stays with its responsible person, because work is assigned to people and delegated to Piloti. The thread reads “Piloti is working on it · for Miriam”. When Piloti is done, it hands the annotation back, however it went. It resolves it only if Miriam asked for that. “Take back from Piloti” takes it back and stops Piloti, and so does resolving, discarding or deleting the annotation.

Piloti never wakes itself, only someone who may edit the document can wake it, and there is a limit per annotation and per person. Waking, too, is something the office switches on.

What happens next lands in the inbox: “Piloti is done with annotation 7”, “Piloti could not finish annotation 7” or “Piloti has a question on annotation 7”. One click opens the review at exactly that annotation. Whoever stopped Piloti themselves gets no item.

## In the chat: a window onto the annotations

What Piloti did on a document in one answer shows up in the chat on one card: proposed and set annotations, changes with the state before and after, replies in threads. The card is not a copy. It reads the annotations as they are now, and reads again whenever one changes. If Miriam took a proposal over in the document, Anna’s card shows it as taken over.

Each row shows its thread at a glance, with the number of replies, who wrote last and the first words of it. With several proposals there is “Take over all” and “Discard the rest”, and discarding asks first. “Show in document” opens the review at the annotation, beside the chat.

## Undo, but never over a colleague

Every change Piloti makes is tied to the answer it came from. That is why it carries “Undo” on the card. Undo works only while the annotation is still as Piloti left it. If Miriam has changed it since, the card undoes nothing and says where the annotation stands now. An annotation Piloti set for Anna, and a reply it wrote for her, can be taken back as long as nobody else has answered in the thread. Otherwise Anna’s click would delete Miriam’s answer with it.

We chose not to build a confirmation before every step. It would have turned “resolve that” into two clicks and added nothing that the note in the thread, undo and steering Piloti while it works do not already give.

## At the end, the minutes

Thursday, site meeting. In the review, Anna exports the minutes as PDF, Word or Markdown, with the open annotations or all of them, for the current revision or all revisions. At the top: the document, “Revision 4 of 4” with its date, the project, and who exported it when. Below that, each annotation by number, with page, kind, status, the person responsible, the due date and the whole thread. What Piloti wrote appears as “Piloti · for Anna”.

Proposals nobody took over do not belong to the office, so they are left out. If you want them anyway, add them under their own heading: “Proposals by Piloti, not reviewed”. The minutes contain no image of the marked place. Each annotation names its page.

## What changes

The review round happens on the document it is about, and the minutes fall out of it instead of being a second job. Piloti sits at the table. You see where it looks, for whom it writes and what it changed, and every change can be taken back as long as nobody has built on it.
